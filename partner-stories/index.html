In employment services, we bring together 12 community organisations. Each keeps its name, its local relationships and its knowledge of the people it serves. We hold the contract and carry the accountability, and our partners deliver in their communities. Partners also share the cost of technology, compliance and training. It’s the same model we’d bring to Support Coordination. Five Bridges and MCM both used it to enter a commissioned program for the first time.

Partner story: Five Bridges

Five Bridges entered a new program, ready from day one

Five Bridges is a Queensland First Nations community organisation. With CoAct as lead, it entered Workforce Australia for the first time as an Indigenous specialist provider, in Fitzroy and Brisbane South. CoAct’s systems, compliance and knowledge transfer meant it could deliver from day one of the contract.

Why it matters for Support Coordination

The consultation paper proposes that specialist and First Nations organisations take part through partnerships. Five Bridges shows this works in practice.

Partner story: MCM

MCM scaled specialist expertise into a government contract

Melbourne City Mission’s (MCM) youth employment service had deep expertise with young people facing disadvantage, but no employment services contract. Through CoAct, MCM secured Inclusive Employment Australia contracts across five Melbourne regions. An 18-month co-designed service model and CoAct’s operational backbone let MCM focus on the young people it supports.

Why it matters for Support Coordination

Participants need specialist expertise. MCM shows how a lead can help a specialist organisation turn that expertise into a government contract, without building the systems alone.
