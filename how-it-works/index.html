How the partnership works

CoAct leads. Partners deliver. Participants keep a choice.

A new market is opening. We could take it on together.

From mid-2028, Support Coordination is set to move to regional contracts. That’s a big shift, and it brings a real opportunity for providers who want to keep doing this work. We see a pathway where we lead and you deliver, sharing the weight and the cost of a government contract rather than carrying them alone.

Government is still working through the detail, so we’ll confirm the final model as the picture becomes clearer.

What the opportunity could mean for you

  • A way into the new market.We’d lead the bid and hold the contract, giving you a pathway into a regional contract you might not reach bidding alone.
  • A more sustainable service.Shared systems and services lower your overheads, so more of every dollar goes to coordination.
  • More time on coordination, less on finding participants.We’re seeking a predictable share of regional demand, so partners can plan their workforce with confidence.
  • Your people at the centre.Your coordinators and the local knowledge that makes you valuable are the reason we want to partner with you.
  • A lighter load.We’d look to take on much of the contract, compliance and reporting work around delivery, so your coordinators can focus on participants.
  • An early conversation.Register interest now and you’ll be among the first to hear how the model is taking shape.

The support we expect to offer

The exact support will depend on government’s final model and funding. Based on how we work today, it’s designed to lower your costs as well as your risk.

Enter

A way into the new market through a CoAct-led bid. We’d bring bid leadership, procurement expertise, financial modelling and service design, so you avoid the cost of bidding alone.

Deliver

A shared service model and practice framework, workforce training through CoAct Academy, practice supervision, and a clear roadmap from contract award to day one. Ready-made frameworks and training mean you don’t pay to build your own.

Protect

We’d carry the contract, compliance and risk. That includes contract and performance management, quality and safeguarding assurance, complaints and incident management, claims and payments, conflict-of-interest support and a collective voice with government. Subject to the final rules, we could manage audit and certification across the network, saving partners the cost and effort of doing it alone.

Connect

Shared technology and data, managed centrally, and the buying power of a network. You get systems and services that would cost far more to fund alone, plus the chance to learn from like-minded providers in a non-competitive network.

The difference

Our model is a genuine service partnership, designed so partners are better off together than going it alone. Providers work together, not against each other, for better outcomes for participants.

Partnering terms that protect participants

Partnering terms will focus on what protects participants and the contract. That means quality and safeguarding, conflict-of-interest compliance, confidentiality, and a supported handover if a partner leaves.

Flexible to government’s final model

Government hasn’t yet confirmed whether it will appoint one lead per region or several providers. It’s also still designing funding and payment arrangements. Our model works either way. We will shape the final offer, including the level of central support, around what government decides and funds.

Local and specialist, side by side

In each region, we expect to bring together generalist partners and specialist partners, such as First Nations, psychosocial, neurological or complex-needs specialists. Partners can join for one region or several.

Who we’re looking for

We want partners who share our purpose and bring something participants need. You don’t need to be big to partner with us. The network gives you scale. We’re partnering with a small number of providers in each region, so every partner has a clear role and participants still have a real choice.

You may be a good fit if you:

  • deliver Support Coordination today, or have strong case-management experience with people with disability
  • have deep roots in one or more regions, with years of local relationships behind you
  • bring specialist capability, such as First Nations, psychosocial, neurological, culturally and linguistically diverse, housing and homelessness, or complex support needs
  • have the staff and capacity to take on more participants
  • are financially sustainable, in good standing with your regulators and funders, and committed to quality and safeguarding
  • want to keep delivering Support Coordination after mid-2028, but don’t want to carry a government contract alone.

If you’re weighing up whether to stay in Support Coordination or step away, talk to us first. Partnership may be the pathway that lets you keep doing the work you’re good at.
