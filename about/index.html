Dear prospective partner,

Support Coordination is changing. Your place in it doesn’t have to.

From 1 July 2028, the Australian Government proposes to commission Support Coordination by region, rather than funding it through participant plans. Today’s open market of around 11,000 support coordinators will become a market of far fewer contracted providers.

For established local and specialist providers, competing for a large regional contract alone is hard, even when you know your participants and your community better than anyone bidding against you.

Prices for Support Coordination haven’t risen since 2019, and we’re not banking on that changing. Sustainability will come from sharing the cost of running a service, not from higher prices. That’s what a network does well.

At CoAct, we believe you shouldn’t have to choose between going it alone and getting out.

For 29 years, CoAct has led government contracts on behalf of community organisations. We hold the contract and carry the accountability. Our partners deliver in their own communities, under their own names. It’s a model built for commissioned markets. One accountable organisation, delivering through local and specialist providers.

As a CoAct partner, you keep your organisation’s identity, your participants’ trust and your local know-how. We bring the tender, the contract, the compliance, the systems and the voice with government.

We’re now talking with a select group of Support Coordination providers about a CoAct bid. We’re not looking to be the biggest. We’re looking for providers whose practice, community connections and regional presence give participants real choice and quality support.

Government is still settling the detail of the new model. But the decisions that shape who stays in this market are happening now. If you want to keep delivering Support Coordination, I’d welcome the conversation.

Simon Brown | Chief Executive Officer, CoAct

Our purpose

To help people create positive, lasting change in their lives and communities. We believe in the power of many. By bringing together our people, partners and communities, we unlock opportunities no one could achieve alone.

We collaborate as one team, and we lead with kindness, respect, inclusion and integrity. That’s how we work with partners, not just what we say.

In Support Coordination, it means participants keep access to coordinators who know them, their supports and their community.

What we bring to Support Coordination

  • A lead provider model that already works.We already run the kind of lead provider model being considered for SCC. One accountable organisation holds the contract. Many local and specialist partners deliver, and participants choose between them.
  • We know how to manage partners, not replace them.Large organisations that win regional contracts tend to deliver most of the work themselves. Our model is built on partners delivering.
  • Continuity for participants.If a delivery partner can no longer deliver, we can support a planned handover to another partner, so participants aren’t left without support.
  • We’re already in Support Coordination.Through CoAct Support Coordination, we’re a registered NDIS provider of Support Coordination.
  • A trusted government contract-holder.We hold contracts with the Australian Government across several programs and manage them to government’s standards for quality, compliance and reporting.

We’re already speaking up for providers

In our submission to the reform consultation in October 2026, we asked government for:

  • a price that covers the full work of coordination, with extra funding for complex needs, SCC+, hard-to-reach areas and transition
  • a predictable share of regional demand, so providers can plan and stay viable
  • fair terms, so delivery partners don’t carry the full risk of a government contract
  • funded transition and contract terms long enough to justify providers’ investment
  • real participant choice of worker and provider, with an independent complaints pathway.

We’ll keep making the case for local and specialist providers as the model is finalised.
